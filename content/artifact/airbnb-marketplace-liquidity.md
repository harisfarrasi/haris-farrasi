Marketplaces are usually judged by total listings or total bookings. That view is too broad to guide product decisions. For Airbnb, the real unit of quality is often much smaller: **city-level liquidity**.

A guest only experiences value if they can find a credible option in the place and time they need. A host only experiences value if demand shows up with enough intent and trust. That means a marketplace can look large overall while still feeling broken in specific local markets.

Airbnb's challenge was not only growth. It was making each new geography feel reliably usable.

## Product Lesson

Two-sided products often scale unevenly. PMs need to know the real local health metric, not only the top-line number that looks good in aggregate.

## What To Study

1. Supply quality versus raw listing count.
2. Review systems as trust bootstrapping.
3. Local density and conversion by geography.
4. Expansion sequencing for two-sided products.
