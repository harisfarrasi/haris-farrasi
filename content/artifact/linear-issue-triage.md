Most people think Linear won because of speed. Snappy UI, instant search, keyboard-first. That's what every review says.

But speed is a feature. **Triage is the product.**

Linear understood something about high-performing teams: the bottleneck isn't writing tickets. It's *deciding what matters*. Every Monday, a team of 10 has 47 open issues. Which 3 get done this sprint? The default behavior of every other tool is to dump everything in a backlog and call it "prioritized." That's cowardice disguised as process.

Linear's triage workflow forces a binary outcome on every incoming issue: **Triaged** or **Backlog**. There's no "maybe." No "let's discuss next sprint." No graveyard of stale tickets that everyone pretends will get done.

What looks like a workflow opinion is actually a structural insight: *the quality of your output is directly proportional to your ruthlessness about input.* Every issue that stays "un-triaged" is a deferred decision. Deferred decisions compound into cognitive debt. A team drowning in "maybe" is a team that never ships.

The brilliance is that they didn't build a feature for triage. They built an *operating system* where not triaging feels worse than triaging. The badge count, the inbox-like interface, the keyboard shortcut muscle memory — it all nudges you toward the hard thing: making a decision *now*.

**The takeaway:** The best products don't optimize for what users say they want. They identify the uncomfortable truth the user is avoiding, and engineer an environment where doing the right thing is easier than doing the comfortable thing.
