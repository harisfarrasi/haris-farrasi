The naive read: Stripe made payments easier. The structural read: Stripe treated the entire global economy as a platform and built the API for it.

Every competitor optimized for the existing market — cheaper fees, better support, more regions. Stripe optimized for *market creation*. Their founding thesis was that the internet should increase the GDP of the world, and payments were the bottleneck preventing that from happening. The goal wasn't to capture payment volume — it was to *unlock transactions that never existed before*.

This is why Stripe does things that look "inefficient": developer experience that costs millions, free Stripe Press books, detailed technical essays, Atlas for company formation. These aren't marketing. They're *capacity-building*. Every developer who loves Stripe's API builds something that creates a transaction that wouldn't exist otherwise. Every founder who incorporates through Atlas adds $1M in potential GDP.

The takeaway: the best platform companies don't ask "how big is the existing market?" They ask "what's the bottleneck preventing a 10x larger market from existing?" Then they build the infrastructure that removes that bottleneck — and capture a fraction of the new value they created.
